# Career analysis from CVs

Use every CV version the user uploads. Output in Hebrew, scannable, about one screen.

## 1. Career map (table)
| years | company | title(s) | industry | level | team / scope | what they mainly did |
Rules: merge promotions in one company into one row with the progression. Note overlaps and gaps.

## 2. What they really do
3 to 5 lines describing the actual work pattern (for example: "most of the time building internal BI products, not running discrete projects"). Titles often hide this.

## 3. Proof
- Achievements with numbers (keep exact wording and numbers).
- Achievements without numbers: ask the user for a number or a concrete outcome.

## 4. Fit: role families
List 4 to 8 role families this background supports, each with:
- why it fits (evidence from the map),
- level that fits (hands-on / lead / manager),
- likely gaps a recruiter would raise and how to frame them honestly.
Include adjacent families the user may not have considered. For career changers, include bridge roles.

## 5. CV review
- Score out of 10 for: clarity, proof (numbers), relevance to target, length and layout, consistency.
- Top 5 fixes, most important first.
- Contradictions between versions (numbers, dates, titles): list them and ask which is true.
- Risky items: personal data that should not be on a CV (ID, marital status, photo, age), negative wording about employers, reasons for leaving.

## 6. Questions for the user
Only what the CVs could not answer.
